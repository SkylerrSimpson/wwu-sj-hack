## 1. Incidents and conflicts

- **2008-01-30, Lummi Nation Fishing Gear Loss Forum.** About 34 people attended: Lummi Natural Resources, BP Cherry Point, USCG Sector Seattle, tug companies and pilots. "Tugs and barges were identified as the primary cause of gear losses." [Lummi forum summary](https://www.lummi-nsn.gov/userfiles/181_Lummi%20Forum%20Meeting%20Summary%5B1%5D.pdf) [read]
- Same forum: tugs waiting for dock space "travel in large circles or figure eights", sometimes "five or more tugs waiting"; another concern was "tugs not anchoring in designated areas, especially when the weather is bad." [Lummi forum summary](https://www.lummi-nsn.gov/userfiles/181_Lummi%20Forum%20Meeting%20Summary%5B1%5D.pdf) [read]
- Same forum: crab pots were Lummi's biggest loss; the highest incidence was "north and south of Alden Bank to the docks at Cherry Point"; October and November were the worst months; "Poaching and bad weather cause a relatively small amount of crab pot losses." [Lummi forum summary](https://www.lummi-nsn.gov/userfiles/181_Lummi%20Forum%20Meeting%20Summary%5B1%5D.pdf) [read]
- Same forum: Lummi asked that "barges should stay out of Lummi Bay"; Hale Passage was named as an area that "could be avoided by agreement"; Lummi suggested shipping-lane enforcement "if transponders were installed to record vessel locations." "No operators interviewed filed incident reports on vessel/pot interactions." [Lummi forum summary](https://www.lummi-nsn.gov/userfiles/181_Lummi%20Forum%20Meeting%20Summary%5B1%5D.pdf) [read]
- **2013-12-13, PRI The World:** "Tankers that service the nearby refinery rip up Lummi crab pots on a regular basis" (the reporter's narration, not a quote). [The World](https://theworld.org/stories/2013/12/13/lummi-fishing-rights) [read]
- **2016-05-09:** the Army Corps (Seattle District, Col. John Buck) denied the Gateway Pacific Terminal permit at Cherry Point. The terminal "would service some 487 bulk carriers a year"; Buck: the Corps "may not permit a project that abrogates treaty rights." It weighed ships only near the dock and trestle. [Cascade PBS](https://www.cascadepbs.org/environment/2016/05/tribal-fishing-rights-net-a-victory-over-coal-plant/) [read]
- The Corps release found impacts to Lummi usual and accustomed fishing rights "greater than de minimis" and denied the permit "without prejudice." [USACE release](https://www.nws.usace.army.mil/Media/News-Releases/Article/754951/army-corps-halts-gateway-pacific-terminal-permitting-process/) [snippet; the page returns 403 to fetchers]
- **2017-02-10:** a USCG proposed rule (USCG-2016-0916) would have formalized Puget Sound anchorages, including Vendovi Island. It records that the tribes' "primary concerns were related to potential restriction of access to Usual & Accustomed fishing grounds," and it proposed that VTS manage anchorages "to avoid or minimize any impact ... on Indian tribal fisheries." [82 FR 10313](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2017-02-10/pdf/2017-02683.pdf) [read]
- **2018-04-27:** the Coast Guard withdrew that proposal "so as to better analyze tribal impacts," after input from tribes. [83 FR 18491](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2018-04-27/pdf/2018-08871.pdf) [snippet]
- **The current federal anchorage, Bellingham Bay General Anchorage:** a circle of 2,000 yards radius centred at 48°44′14.39″N, 122°32′26.62″W. The Explosives Anchorage is a circle of 1,000 yards at 48°42′47.39″N, 122°33′41.62″W. [33 CFR 110.230](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-33/chapter-I/subchapter-A/part-110/subpart-B/section-110.230) [read, via the eCFR API]
- Both anchorage centres are outside the Census reservation polygon. The General Anchorage's edge is about **0.8 km** from the boundary, and the Explosives Anchorage's edge is about 2.1 km from it. [computed from 33 CFR 110.230 and `lummi-reservation-census.geojson`]
- **2019-02-13:** the Port of Bellingham and the Lummi Nation reached an agreement resolving issues from changes to Bellingham Bay and Drayton Harbor within Lummi usual and accustomed grounds; it gives the Lummi fishing fleet moorage in Squalicum and Blaine Harbors. [Port of Bellingham](https://portofbellingham.com/CivicAlerts.aspx?AID=283) [read]
- **2019-02-25:** the Lummi Nation's statement on Trans Mountain: tankers would rise from about 60 to over 400 a year; Lummi called for a moratorium on new or increased Salish Sea shipping until a transboundary cumulative impact assessment is done. [ICT press pool](https://ictnews.org/the-press-pool/lummi-nations-response-to-the-national-energy-boards-recommendation-to-approve-the-trans-mountain-pipeline/) [read]
- The Trans Mountain expansion began commercial operations on 2024-05-01; 22 tankers were scheduled to load at Westridge in June 2024. [BOE Report / Reuters](https://boereport.com/2024/06/12/trans-mountain-pipeline-running-80-full-to-load-22-oil-tankers-in-vancouver) [snippet]
- **Tanker traffic in 2024:** 982 tank-ship entering transits into Washington waters, of which 352 were bound for Canadian ports and 39 for Puget Sound ports via the Strait of Georgia and Haro Strait. [Ecology VEAT 2024, pub. 25-08-008](https://apps.ecology.wa.gov/publications/documents/2508008.pdf) [read]
- **2024-01-21:** BP planned to buy 1,100 acres at Xwe'chi'eXen (Cherry Point), described as "a historic village, fishing grounds and final resting place." [Seattle Times via The Columbian](https://columbian.com/news/2024/jan/21/bp-bought-a-sacred-place-now-lummi-nation-is-preparing-again-to-fend-off-development) [read]
- **The state's own rule on non-tribal fishing:** WDFW's 2026-27 Marine Area 7 rules list "Lummi Bay (waters east of a line from Gooseberry Point to Sandy Point) AND Reservation Closed to non-Indian fishing and/or access." [WDFW eRegulations, Marine Area 7](https://eregulations.com/washington/fishing/marine-area-7) [read]
- A 2011 WDFW emergency rule closed Lummi Bay to salmon fishing "to reduce potential conflict between recreational anglers and tribal fishers." [WDFW 2011](https://wdfw.wa.gov/fishing/regulations/emergency-rules/lummi-bay-salmon-closure-2011-09) [snippet]
- **Gap:** I found no public news report or court record of a specific non-tribal vessel caught fishing inside the reservation. Ask the rep.

## 2. Who enforces, under what authority, and what evidence

- Lummi Natural Resource Enforcement Officers sit in the Lummi Nation Police Department and patrol "usual and accustomed fishing and hunting areas, by patrol vehicles and vessels." Their work falls under Title 10.19 (Natural Resources Enforcement Patrol) and Title 9 (Law Enforcement Officers Code). [Lummi job document](https://www.lummi-nsn.gov/widgets/JobDocument.php?id=4065) [snippet]
- The public Lummi Code compilation (Titles 9 Law Enforcement, 10 Natural Resources, 12 Exclusion, 13 Tidelands) was last updated in March 2019, and "official versions must be requested from the tribe." The title PDFs now return 404. [NILL Lummi code index](https://narf.org/nill/codes/lummi/index.html) [read]
- The courts confirm the tidelands are in the reservation. In 2010 the Supreme Court declined _Sharp v. United States_, leaving the Ninth Circuit's holding that "the reservation continues to include the tidelands on the Strait of Georgia" (a federal trespass action against non-Indian lessees). [Indianz, 2010-05-17](https://indianz.com/News/2010/019812.asp) [read]
- _Oliphant v. Suquamish_ (1978): tribes "lack inherent sovereign power to exercise criminal jurisdiction over non-Indians." _Montana v. United States_ (1981): tribal powers generally "do not extend to the activities of nonmembers," with exceptions, including conduct that "threatens or has some direct effect on ... the health or welfare of the tribe." [Cooley opinion restating both](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/20pdf/593us2r40_j5fl.pdf) [read]
- _United States v. Cooley_ (2021-06-01, unanimous): "A tribal police officer has authority to detain temporarily and to search non-Indian persons traveling on public rights-of-way running through a reservation for potential violations of state or federal law." It rests on Montana's second exception, and officers may "detain the offender and transport him to the proper authorities." [Cooley, 593 U.S. 345](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/20pdf/593us2r40_j5fl.pdf) [read]. _Cooley_ concerned a highway; I found no case applying it on reservation waters.
- Under RCW 10.92.020, tribal officers who meet the insurance and training conditions act as general authority Washington peace officers, with authority "coextensive with the exterior boundaries of the reservation." Citations to non-Indians go "to a Washington court," a copy goes to the sheriff "within three days," and the statute "shall not in any way expand the jurisdiction of any tribal court." [RCW 10.92.020](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=10.92.020) [read]. Whether Lummi officers hold this status: not found.
- Lummi Code 1.02.030 treats entering the reservation as consent to Tribal Court jurisdiction (civil). [Lummi Title 1](https://nill.narf.org/codes/lummi/1Court.pdf) [snippet]
- **USCG:** vessels with AIS must broadcast accurate identity, position and navigational status; a violation carries civil penalties "not to exceed $25,000 (46 U.S.C. 70119)." [NAVCEN VIVS FAQ](https://navcen.uscg.gov/node/579) [read]. A 2021 report gives up to $35,486 per violation. [MarineLink 2021-01-20](https://www.marinelink.com/news/us-coast-guard-alarmed-mariners-turning-484700) [read]
- **Evidence:** "there have been no cases in which illegal fishing activity was proven in a court of law using AIS data alone." AIS "can be used to prove where a vessel was and at what time," and it is "an essential piece of evidence, in conjunction with other documents." [Stop Illegal Fishing 2018](https://stopillegalfishing.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/reports-the-potential-use-of-ais-fisheries-as-a-monitoring-tool-english.pdf) [read]
- A 2001 US case was the first federal fisheries prosecution based only on satellite VMS tracks; the vessel's position in the closed area had to be proved, not the fishing. [NOAA 2001 release, archived](https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/eot2008/20080916064048/http:/www.publicaffairs.noaa.gov/releases2001/dec01/noaa01r153.html) [snippet]

## 3. Prior art

- **Skylight (Ai2):** "free to government, regional, and nongovernmental organizations worldwide." It has a US Coast Guard testimonial; no tribal users are mentioned. [skylight.global](https://www.skylight.global/) [read]
- Skylight events: **Entry** ("first AIS signal received" inside an area of interest) and **Speed Range**, both needing an area of interest; Standard Rendezvous (two AIS vessels within 250 m for more than 30 min); Dark Rendezvous; Fishing; and vessel detections from satellite radar, night lights, RF and optical imagery. [Skylight help: events](https://skylight.helpjuice.com/en_US/what-are-events) [read]
- **Global Fishing Watch caveats:** "Gap events less than 50 nautical miles from shore are unreliable"; Class B's "less frequent ping rate" lowers coverage; SAR "miss[es] most vessels under 15 m"; "We do not classify objects within 1 km of shore." [GFW data caveats](https://globalfishingwatch.org/our-apis/documentation/docs/v3/general-api-doc/data-caveats) [read]
- GFW is licensed for non-commercial use (CC BY-NC 4.0), and the API's rights are revocable. [GFW license](https://api-doc.globalfishingwatch.org/our-apis/documentation/docs/license-rate-limits) [snippet]. Its Marine Manager portal is free. [National Fisherman](https://www.nationalfisherman.com/national-international/global-fishing-watch-marine-manager-reaches-a-new-milestone) [snippet]
- **OceanMind** "has worked side by side with governments" using satellite data, AI and analysts. Its site gives no pricing. [OceanMind](https://www.oceanmind.global/home) [read]
- **MarineTraffic and VesselFinder:** I did not find or read their terms. Assume no redistribution until checked.
- **Transport Canada's Enhanced Maritime Situational Awareness (EMSA)** launched in May 2019 in 10 Indigenous communities, including T'Sou-ke, Pacheedaht, Scia'new, Malahat and Tseycum on south Vancouver Island. It combines terrestrial and satellite AIS with "local knowledge," sensitive areas and cultural sites. [Transport Canada](https://tc.canada.ca/en/marine-transportation/navigation-marine-conditions/enhanced-maritime-situational-awareness-initiative-pilot-projects) [read]
- In a May 2024 exercise with T'Sou-ke, Tsawout, Sc'ianew, Tsartlip and Malahat, the nations used BRNKL remote monitoring for wrecked, abandoned and hazardous vessels. [ECO Magazine](https://ecomagazine.com/news/coastal/brnkl-rapid-deploy-used-to-empower-first-nations-in-monitoring-traditional-waters/) [read]
- **Coastal First Nations Guardian Watchmen:** their "continual presence on the water encourages compliance"; a Regional Monitoring System is free to member nations. [CFN FAQ](https://coastalfirstnations.ca/our-stewardship/cgw-resources/cgw-faq) [snippet]
- The USCG Puget Sound VTS Cetacean Desk was built with input from the Makah, Port Gamble S'Klallam and Lummi Nation. [Puget Sound Partnership](https://makingwaves.psp.wa.gov/?p=5659) [snippet]

[Lummi forum summary](https://www.lummi-nsn.gov/userfiles/181_Lummi%20Forum%20Meeting%20Summary%5B1%5D.pdf) [read]

## 4. AIS limitations

- **Class A** reports every 2 to 10 s underway and every 3 min at anchor, at 12.5 W. Navigational status codes: 0 under way using engine, 1 at anchor, 5 moored, 7 engaged in fishing. [NAVCEN Class A](https://www.navcen.uscg.gov/ais-class-a-reports) [read]
- **Class B** reports every 30 s at 2 to 14 kn and every 3 min at 2 kn or less, at 2 W for CS units. Static data comes every 6 min, and Class B sends no voyage data. [NAVCEN class comparison](https://navcen.uscg.gov/sites/default/files/pdf/AIS_Comparison_By_Class.pdf), [NAVCEN types of AIS](https://navcen.uscg.gov/types-of-ais) [read]
- Navigational status is set by hand: "Navigation Status must always be up-to-date." [USCG AIS Encoding Guide](https://navcen.uscg.gov/sites/default/files/pdf/AIS/USCG_AIS_Encoding_Guide.pdf) [snippet]
- In June 2018, "over 50 percent of towing vessels operating in U.S. waters transmitted incorrect AIS data," including wrong dimensions and MMSI numbers that were not properly assigned. [Professional Mariner](https://professionalmariner.com/coast-guard-50-percent-of-us-towing-vessels-transmitting-incorrect-ais-data/) [read]
- **MMSI:** the USCG cannot verify an MMSI; a used radio can carry its old MMSI; and the boat name lives in the radio, so a sold boat can broadcast its old name. [NAVCEN MMSI FAQ](https://navcen.uscg.gov/node/41623) [read]
- AIS must run "at all times that the vessel is navigating (underway or at anchor)," but it may be switched off when its use would compromise safety, with a report to the Captain of the Port. [NAVCEN VIVS FAQ](https://navcen.uscg.gov/node/579) [read]
- **Going dark:** in January 2021 the USCG was alarmed by fishing and crabbing boats switching off AIS, reportedly to hide their spots from competitors. [MarineLink](https://www.marinelink.com/news/us-coast-guard-alarmed-mariners-turning-484700) [read]
- A 2022 _Science Advances_ study found over 55,000 suspected intentional disabling events from 2017 to 2019, obscuring up to 6% of fishing-vessel activity; some disabling is legitimate. [UCSC news](https://news.ucsc.edu/2022/11/unseen-fishing/) [snippet]
- **Spoofing:** "1-2 % of all ships report fake vessel names and identification numbers on their AIS." [Stop Illegal Fishing 2018](https://stopillegalfishing.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/reports-the-potential-use-of-ais-fisheries-as-a-monitoring-tool-english.pdf) [read]
- **Coverage:** USCG NAIS receivers cover out to about 50 nm offshore (2008 fact sheet). [NAIS fact sheet, archived](https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/eot2008/20080921183555/http:/uscg.mil/nais/documents/NAIS_Fact_Sheet.pdf) [snippet]. NAVCEN delivers raw historical NAIS data to MarineCadastre. [USCG CI 5230.80A](https://navcen.uscg.gov/sites/default/files/pdf/AIS/CI5230_80A.pdf) [snippet]. I found nothing specific to Bellingham.
- **Invisible vessels:** towed barges have no AIS of their own ("the vessel engaged in towing will show an AIS signal"), and tribal fishing vessels were modeled as a "non-AIS" vessel type, each tribe's boats returning to its own docks. [Ecology technical session, 2020-11-04](https://ecology.wa.gov/Asset-Collections/Doc-Assets/Spills/Preventing-spills/November-4,-2020-Vessels-that-do-not-transmit-AIS) [read]

## 5. Indigenous data sovereignty

- **CARE Principles** (Global Indigenous Data Alliance, 2019): Collective benefit, Authority to control, Responsibility, Ethics. "Indigenous Peoples' rights and interests in Indigenous data must be recognised and their authority to control such data must be empowered." CARE is meant to complement FAIR, not replace it. [GIDA CARE](https://www.gida-global.org/care) [snippet; the page 404'd when fetched]
- **OCAP®** (Ownership, Control, Access, Possession; 1998) is a registered trademark of the First Nations Information Governance Centre, and it is Canada-specific. [Wikipedia: OCAP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Nations_principles_of_OCAP) [snippet; fnigc.ca blocked fetches]. In a US context, cite CARE and name OCAP only as related.
- **The UW thesis:** Amelia Schwartz, _Indigenous Nations' Access to Geospatial Climate Change Data: The case of the Lummi Nation_, MA Geography, 2020, with the embargo lifted 2025-07-19. It calls the work "a contemporary approach to data sovereignty" and argues agencies should provide accessible data without requiring reciprocity, honoring the Treaty of Point Elliott. [UW ResearchWorks, hdl 1773/46020](https://digital.lib.washington.edu/researchworks/items/d34800a2-29b1-4f27-b9c6-5b13f2463cd5/full) [read: abstract only; the PDF returned 403]
- The **Lummi GIS Division** "is responsible for developing, documenting, and maintaining spatial information for all Lummi Indian Business Council departments." Consultants request other data through a "Lummi geospatial data disclaimer form." [LNR GIS page](https://lumminaturalresources.org/Page.php?p=23) [read]
- **The Nation publishes its own boundary:** "Reservation Boundary Layer Shapefile," `LummiReservationBoundary.zip`, 11 KB, uploaded 2025-09-24. [LNR document f=72](https://lumminaturalresources.org/document.php?f=72) [read: downloaded to scratch, inspected and deleted]
  - It is the "combined upland and tideland boundary": one ring, 97 vertices, Web Mercator, metadata dated 2013-12-02. Its box matches the Census polygon, and its area is about 97 km², against the Census polygon's 97.4 km² of land plus water. [computed]
  - The metadata says it is "approximate" and does "not reflect the legal extent of the Lummi Reservation," with credit to the "LIBC Department of Natural Resources."
  - Its access constraint reads **"No part of this document may be reproduced without prior consent of the Lummi Nation."**

## Implications for the build

1. **The documented harm is mostly commercial traffic, not poachers.** Lummi's own 2008 forum names tugs and barges circling, waiting and anchoring over crab gear as the primary cause of loss, and poaching as a small share. Show every vessel inside, with dwell and anchoring time, not just "unapproved fishing boats." Ask the rep which threat the officer means.
2. **Boundary:** keep the Census polygon in the public build. The Nation's own upland-and-tideland layer exists but says "No part ... may be reproduced without prior consent." Ask the rep for consent; if it is given, the layer is loaded at deploy time only. Label either layer "approximate, not a legal boundary."
3. **Wording:** an officer cannot cite a non-Indian into tribal court (_Oliphant_; RCW 10.92 sends citations to a Washington court). The output is therefore a **referral record**, not a verdict. Say "entered Lummi waters (approximate boundary)" and "not on the approved list," never "illegal" or "intruder."
4. **What a record must contain, since AIS alone has never proven a fishing case in court:**
   - the broadcast identity (MMSI, name, type, size), labeled "self-reported"
   - the UTC and local time of each fix inside the boundary
   - the first and last fix inside, and the time between them
   - how far inside the boundary it went
   - any gaps in the fixes
   - the data source with its retrieval date, and the boundary's source and version
   - a line saying "corroborate with a sighting or photo before acting"
5. **Detecting anchoring:** navigational status is set by hand and often wrong. Both Class A at anchor and Class B at 2 kn or less report only every 3 min, so 10 min of low speed is about 3 fixes. Use speed over a time window, show the status as given, and mark which rule fired.
6. **Gaps:** a gap inside the boundary is "no AIS received," not "went dark." Crabbers switch off for legitimate reasons too, and coverage near shore is uneven. Barges and most small and tribal boats never appear, so the on-screen caveat stays.
7. **Near-boundary noise:** the Bellingham Bay General Anchorage sits about 0.8 km outside the boundary, and the Cherry Point docks are inside the usual and accustomed area but outside the reservation. Expect swinging and arriving ships near the edge, and consider a minimum time or depth inside before showing a visit as flagged.
